# Design Context

### Users

QCU (Quezon City University) students on phones, checking one thing under time
pressure: **"do I have class today?"** Peak usage is early morning during the
rainy season, often while already deciding whether to leave the house. Secondary
context: mid-day, checking whether an afternoon suspension just landed.

The job to be done is a *decision*, not browsing. Every surface should answer the
question in one glance and then get out of the way.

### Brand Personality

**Institutional · truthful · calm.**

This is a public-service status board, not a consumer app. It borrows the voice of
a government advisory: plain, specific, unexcited. It never celebrates, never
alarms beyond what the facts support, and never pads.

The non-negotiable rule, already encoded throughout `assets/js/status.js`:
**a false "no suspension" is worse than an honest "unavailable."** Any data
failure must read as unknown, never as clear. UI copy must never imply a
confirmation the data does not support.

### Aesthetic Direction

Sharp / enterprise flat, light mode only (`color-scheme: light`), established
across ~1600 lines of `assets/css/styles.css`:

- 1px hairline borders; 2–4px radii; flat surfaces; **no drop shadows** on status
  surfaces (`box-shadow: none` is set explicitly)
- Uppercase 10–11px micro-labels with 0.05–0.1em tracking for kickers and keys
- `Public Sans` throughout; weight (600/700/800) carries hierarchy, not color
- Functional status color, used sparingly and only to mean something:
  `--fn-red` suspended · `--fn-amber` partial · `--fn-green` normal ·
  `--fn-blue` weather/advisory. The QCU institutional palette (`--blue`,
  `--navy`, `--gold`) stays reserved for chrome and identity.
- `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on every timestamp, count, and metric
- Mobile-first single column that fans into a grid at 560px; must survive 320px
  with no horizontal scroll and no clipped text

**Theme rationale:** light mode is correct here and is not a default retreat —
this is consulted outdoors, in daylight, on a phone, often one-handed while
walking. Dark mode would lose legibility in exactly the conditions that matter
most.

**Anti-references:** consumer weather apps (gradients, glass, illustrated
mascots), dashboard templates (identical card grids, decorative sparklines),
and anything that makes a normal day look like a celebration.

### Design Principles

1. **One question, one answer.** Each surface states its verdict once, in plain
   language. If a chip already carries the machine label, the body must not
   repeat it in different words.
2. **Never fabricate; degrade honestly.** Missing data produces a neutral
   "unknown" state, never an implied all-clear. Counts and metrics are omitted
   rather than shown as zero when the source is unavailable.
3. **Separate our reading from their words.** Our interpretation ("Classes are in
   session") and the official announcement's own text live in visually distinct
   layers so they can never be confused.
4. **Only alerts get filled color.** Filled status color means *act on this*.
   Calm and unknown states are outlined or neutral, so a normal day never shouts.
5. **Earn trust by showing the work.** On a quiet day, the reassurance *is* the
   visible fact that the official source was checked — that belongs in the card
   body, not buried in a footer.
